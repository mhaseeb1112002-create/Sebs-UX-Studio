A long sign-up isn't what loses people. Not knowing how long it is does.

Fintech sign-ups have to be long: identity, address and occupation checks are required. So the question isn't how to remove steps. It's how to keep people moving through them. Here is how I'd judge Wise's personal sign-up on the web.

## 1. Count what a new user sees

- First screen: "First, enter your email address", with Google, Facebook and Apple sign-up.
- "Check your email", then "Email confirmed".
- Account type, with a progress bar: Account type · Country · 2FA · Password.
- Then the bar disappears, and 5 more screens follow: your details (country, legal name, date of birth), your address (search, manual entry, confirm) and your occupation.

## 2. Keep what already works

**One field to start**, or one tap with Google or Apple.

**It says why it asks.** On the address screen: "We may need to ask for proof of this address." People know what's coming, so it doesn't feel like a surprise later.

**Home gives you a next step.** "Welcome to Wise" offers three clear actions: add a currency, spend and withdraw abroad, or get account details to get paid.

## 3. Find where people lose their place

When the progress bar vanishes, nobody can answer the question every new user has: how much longer? The last 5 screens feel longer than they are, and that's where people put it off "for later".

## 4. Fix that moment, and only that

Keep the progress bar to the end, and show the time left:

> Step 2 of 4 · About 2 minutes left

The regulatory steps stay exactly as they are. People just know where they are.

## Why this matters

That's the difference between a redesign and an audit. You keep what converts and fix the step that doesn't.

*Flow reviewed in November 2026 from a recorded web sign-up; Wise may have changed it since. The fix is a concept, not Wise's design.*
