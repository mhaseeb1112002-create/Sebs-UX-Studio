Most teams guess where their sign-up loses people. Then they redesign the screens they dislike most.

Sign-ups don't move, because people were quitting somewhere else.

The Drop-off Method is how I avoid that. It has three steps, always in this order.

## 01 Find the step

Map every step of the flow, from the first click to the first useful moment. Then count how many people reach each step.

| Step | People who reach it (example) |
|---|---|
| Sign up | 1,000 |
| Verify email | 820 |
| Company details | 410 |
| Connect bank | 370 |
| First payment | 300 |

*Example numbers, not client data.*

Here the biggest drop is between "Verify email" and "Company details": half the people leave. That is the step to work on. Everything else waits.

You can do this yourself in about 10 minutes with any analytics tool that shows a funnel.

## 02 Prove the why

A funnel tells you **where** people leave. It doesn't tell you **why**.

To find the why, I use whatever the product has:

- **Analytics** for patterns: device, traffic source, time spent on the step.
- **Session recordings** to watch real attempts.
- **5 real people** trying the flow while thinking out loud.

Five people is enough to see the biggest problems. It isn't a statistic. It's a way to stop guessing.

## 03 Fix that step first

Design the fix for that one step, and agree how to measure it **before** anyone builds it. For example: "More than 60% of people who reach Company details finish it."

Then ship, measure, and move to the next biggest drop.

## Why the order matters

Most redesigns start at step 3. They fix screens without knowing whether those screens were the problem.

The steps are simple. The order is the point.

## Use it on your own sign-up

1. Open your sign-up funnel in analytics.
2. Write down how many people reach each step.
3. Circle the biggest drop.
4. Watch 5 people try that step.
5. Fix what you saw, and decide how you'll measure it.
