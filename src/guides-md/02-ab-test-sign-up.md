Your sign-up converts 2% of visitors. You've redesigned a screen and want to A/B test it.

Before you do, check how many visitors you'll need.

## The number most early teams don't have

To trust a 15% lift on a 2% conversion rate (2% → 2.3%), a standard test needs about **36,700 visitors per version**. That's roughly 73,000 visitors for one test.

*How I got this: a two-sided sample-size calculation at 95% confidence and 80% power.*

| Your conversion | Lift you want to detect | Visitors per version |
|---|---|---|
| 2% | 15% | ~36,700 |
| 2% | 10% | ~80,700 |
| 5% | 15% | ~14,200 |

Most early SaaS and fintech teams wait months to reach that. Many stop the test early, and early results are often wrong.

## A test tells you who won, not why

Even when the test finishes, you learn which version won. You don't learn why people were quitting. So the next idea is another guess.

## Watch 5 people first

Before you test, watch 5 people try your sign-up while they think out loud. In one afternoon you'll see where they hesitate, what they misread, and where they give up.

Five people won't measure a problem. They'll show you which problem to work on, so the change you finally test is the right one.

## When A/B testing makes sense

- You have enough traffic for the lift you care about (use the table above).
- You already know **why** people quit at that step.
- You're choosing between two good options, not looking for the problem.

## The order that works

1. **Find the step** with the biggest drop in your funnel.
2. **Watch 5 people** go through it.
3. **Fix** what you saw.
4. **Then test**, if your traffic allows it.

This is step 2 of the [Drop-off Method](/guides/drop-off-method/): prove the why before you change anything.
