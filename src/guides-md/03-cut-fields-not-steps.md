When a sign-up feels long, the usual fix is to merge screens. Fourteen fields on five screens become fourteen fields on two.

It looks shorter. It isn't.

## Fields matter more than steps

Baymard Institute studies checkout forms at scale. Their research found that the number of form fields affects completion more than the number of steps. The average checkout they reviewed had **11.3 fields**, while most could work with about **8**.

Sign-up works the same way. Every field is something people have to read, think about and type. Squeezing them onto fewer screens makes each screen heavier.

## What to cut first

**1. Split name fields.** In Baymard's testing, 42% of users typed their full name into a "First name" field. One "Full name" field avoids the problem.

**2. Optional fields shown by default.** "Address line 2", "Company website", "How did you hear about us?" Hide them behind a link, or remove them.

**3. Anything you can prefill.** Country from location, company from email domain, time zone from the browser.

**4. Anything you can ask later.** Role, team size and phone number are rarely needed before someone sees value. Ask when the answer changes what they see.

## A quick test for every field

For each field in your sign-up, write when you actually need it:

- **Now**: you can't create the account without it.
- **Later**: you need it, but not before the first win.
- **Never**: nobody uses it.

Keep "Now". Move "Later" to after the first useful screen. Delete "Never".

## When more steps are fine

Splitting a long form into short, clear steps can help, as long as each step has a few fields and people can see how many steps are left. Steps aren't the enemy. Unneeded fields are.
