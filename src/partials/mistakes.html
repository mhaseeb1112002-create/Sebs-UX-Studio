Mistakes I see

8 mistakes that quietly lose users

Most agencies list services. I’d rather show you what goes wrong, what people see, and the fix.

  • Fintech

    Asking for an ID photo with no guidance

    What people seePeople don’t know what counts as a good photo, so they retry. Name or address mismatches only show up after a rejection.

    Do this insteadShow an example photo, lighting tips and the matching rules before the camera opens.

  • Fintech

    Rejecting with “something went wrong”

    What people seeA message like “an issue with your identity verification” and nothing else.

    Do this insteadSay what failed, give one next step, and keep the person’s progress.

  • Fintech

    Leaving no way out

    What people seeRetry loops that end at a support form.

    Do this insteadOffer another document, a human chat, or a way to come back later.

  • SaaS

    Mistaking setup for value

    What people seeProfile photos, team invites and an empty dashboard before anything useful happens.

    Do this insteadPick one first win and show it before the setup.

  • SaaS

    One onboarding for everyone, then silence

    What people seeEvery new user gets the same tour, and nobody follows up after login.

    Do this insteadAsk one question to route people, and prompt them at the moment they stall.

  • SaaS

    Asking for too much before showing value

    What people seeLong forms and forced accounts before anyone has seen what the product does.

    Do this insteadAsk only what the next step needs, and let people look around first.

  • B2B

    A website that tells a different story from sales

    What people see69% of B2B buyers report that the website and the sellers disagree (Gartner, 2025).

    Do this insteadKeep one story from the first page to the demo call, and offer a smaller first step than a demo.

  • Any product

    Blaming the page when the problem is who arrives

    What people seeTeams redesign a page that is fine while the wrong visitors keep arriving.

    Do this insteadCheck who is arriving and why before changing screens. A good audit tells you when the answer isn’t design.
